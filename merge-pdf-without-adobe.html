The Reader trap

Adobe Acrobat Reader is free, and it will not combine two PDFs. The menu item exists in some builds and does nothing useful without a subscription, which is where a lot of people give up and assume merging is a paid feature everywhere.

Merging lives in Acrobat Standard and Acrobat Pro, both subscription products. If you already pay for Acrobat at work, use it. If you need to merge four invoices once, paying a monthly fee is a poor trade.

What is already on the machine

On macOS, Preview merges PDFs with no extra software. Open the first document, show the sidebar, drag the rest in, then save. It handles a few dozen files before the thumbnail dragging becomes tedious.

On Windows there is no built-in merger. Printing to Microsoft Print to PDF creates one file per print job, so it cannot combine documents. The usual answer is PDFsam Basic, free and open source, which asks for Java first. LibreOffice Draw opens PDFs and can export a merged file, but it treats a PDF as an editable drawing, and complicated layouts will shift.

Merging in the browser

A browser tool avoids the install and the subscription. The page loads once and then does the merge in the tab, so the documents stay on your machine.

Merge a PDF in the browser, free

This is the shortest path on a locked-down laptop or a Chromebook. It is not a replacement for Acrobat when the job involves more than combining files, and the next section is about exactly that.

Where Acrobat is genuinely better

Three jobs come to mind, and no browser tool I know of does them well. Optical character recognition on a scanned document: Acrobat reads the text layer and makes it searchable, which a browser tab cannot match for large files. Fillable forms and digital signatures: Acrobat handles certificate-based signing and form fields that other tools flatten or drop. PDF/A and accessibility remediation: if you submit to a court, a regulator or an archive with format requirements, Acrobat validates against the profile.

If your work is one of those, the subscription is not the problem to solve. If your work is combining documents, you are paying for a printing press to staple two pages together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Adobe Acrobat Reader merge PDF files for free?

No. Acrobat Reader opens and annotates PDFs, but combining files requires Acrobat Standard or Pro, both paid subscriptions.

What is the easiest free way to merge PDFs on Windows?

Install PDFsam Basic, which is free and open source, or use a browser tool that needs no install. Windows itself has no built-in merger.

Can I merge PDFs on Mac without paying?

Yes. Preview is built into macOS and merges files: open the first PDF, show the sidebar, drag the other documents into it, then save.

Does merging in the browser lose quality?

No. The pages are copied into a new document rather than re-rendered, so text stays selectable and images keep their original resolution.

When should I pay for Acrobat instead?

When the job needs OCR on scans, fillable forms, certificate-based signatures, or PDF/A compliance for a court or archive. For simply combining documents, a free tool does the same thing.
